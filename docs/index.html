01 · Question

Which signals actually predict search ranking performance?

Content teams routinely make prioritization decisions based on intuition: target high search-volume keywords, write longer articles, publish frequently. This work tests those assumptions against real performance data.

Research question: Which content and search performance signals are most predictive of a page achieving and sustaining a high search ranking position — and which commonly assumed signals have no real predictive power?

Decision this supports: Given a portfolio of content pages, which signals should a content team optimize first to improve ranking tier and arrest declining trends?

Output: A ranked signal importance report (SHAP) and an evidence-backed action playbook — six recommendations ordered by predictive power.

What we excluded and why

The following columns directly encode or are derived from the outcome label and were excluded as features to prevent leakage:

Product flags: health_score, needs_ctr_fix, is_quick_win, needs_indexing, needs_engagement_fix, ai_opportunity, is_underperformer, is_declining, is_initial_refresh_candidate

Position/trend encodings: ctr, avg_position, impression_tier — these directly encode position_tier which was used to build the target.

Data quality: scroll_rate, ai_traffic_pct — contain values >100 indicating measurement issues.

03 · Methodology

Target definition

is_top_performer = 1 when position_tier ∈ {top_3, page_1} AND trend_direction ∈ {up, stable, flat}. This captures both current ranking quality and momentum — a page ranking well but declining is not a top performer.

Feature engineering

Seven derived ratio features added to the 38 clean raw columns:

impression_momentum      = impressions_last_30d / (impressions_prev_30d + 1)
click_momentum           = clicks_last_30d / (clicks_prev_30d + 1)
click_through_efficiency = clicks_90d / (impressions_90d + 1)
engagement_density       = engaged_sessions_90d / (sessions_90d + 1)
session_per_click        = sessions_90d / (clicks_90d + 1)
content_density          = char_count / (word_count + 1)
ai_traffic_share         = ai_sessions_90d / (sessions_90d + 1)

Leakage-safe split

GroupShuffleSplit on client_id (test_size=0.2, random_state=42). No client appears in both train and test sets — prevents the model from learning client-specific patterns that wouldn't generalize to new clients. Train: 23,837 rows · Test: 6,163 rows.

Model

RandomForestClassifier — n_estimators=50, max_depth=6, min_samples_leaf=20, class_weight='balanced', random_state=42. Chosen for interpretability via SHAP and robustness to mixed feature types.

Primary metric: Precision@50 — top 50 predicted positives. Actionable for a content team: you act on the pages the model is most confident about.

Assumptions: All ratios use +1 smoothing to avoid division by zero. Null values in keyword metrics (search_volume, competition, cpc) filled with 0 — 8.2% null rate, indicating clients without keyword tool access.

05 · Limitations & honest framing

What this work cannot claim

Observational, not causal. We identify correlates of top performance, not causes. Pages that already rank well naturally accumulate more impressions and clicks, inflating impression-based features. Reverse causality is likely for the top signals.

Momentum dominance. The #1 signal (SHAP 0.1418) dwarfs all others (next: 0.0312). This gap suggests the model is largely learning "pages already growing continue to grow" — a momentum effect that is observable but not directly actionable as a lever to pull.

Engagement signals are flat. engagement_rate and engagement_density both have median = 0 across top and bottom performers. These signals carry no useful information in this dataset.

Word count is not a differentiator. Top performers: 2,797 words median. Bottom performers: 2,902. A 4% difference in the wrong direction.

Fixed 90-day window. All performance metrics are aggregated over a single snapshot. We cannot observe how signals change over time or model causal sequences.

No content topic or vertical. Fully anonymized — no URLs, titles, or keywords. We cannot control for industry vertical or SERP feature type, both of which affect CTR and position independently of content signals.

impression_momentum HIGH
#1 signal by 4.5× margin (SHAP 0.1418). Pages with last_30d impressions > prev_30d are far more likely to be top performers. 3,228 pages are growing fast but still below median impressions.
→ Identify pages with impression_momentum > 1.2 and below median impressions. These are your highest-leverage targets. Refresh content, add internal links, expand topically to sustain growth.
2
impressions volume HIGH
Raw impression signals (impressions_90d, last_30d, prev_30d) occupy ranks 2, 3, and 11. Top performers have 2.36× higher median impressions than bottom performers.
→ Pages with low impressions_90d but positive momentum are growing but not yet visible. Prioritize these for link building and topical authority expansion.
3
click_momentum + clicks_90d HIGH
Click signals occupy ranks 5, 6, and 9. Click growth predicts sustained top performance independently of impression volume.
→ Monitor clicks_last_30d vs clicks_prev_30d monthly. Pages where impressions are growing but clicks are flat need CTR optimization — test title and meta description variants.
4
click_through_efficiency HIGH
2,522 pages (8.4% of all content) have >500 impressions but zero clicks. Visible but completely failing to capture traffic.
→ Flag all pages with impressions_90d > 500 and clicks_90d = 0. Fix titles and meta descriptions first. These are immediate wins requiring no content rewrite.
5
days_since_last_update MEDIUM
Rank 14 in SHAP but operationally important — stale content correlates with declining trend direction.
→ Build a freshness queue: flag all pages not updated in 60+ days with flat or declining impression_momentum for immediate review and refresh.
6
search_volume + word_count STRATEGIC
search_volume r = 0.0012 with impressions. Word count median: 2,797 (top) vs 2,902 (bottom). Both are myths — neither predicts ranking in this dataset.
→ Remove search_volume and word count targets from content briefs. Replace with impression_momentum and click_through_efficiency as the primary prioritization signals.
